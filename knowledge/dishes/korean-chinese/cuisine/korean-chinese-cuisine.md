# Korean-Chinese cuisine

## Overview
Korean-Chinese cuisine is a Korean dining tradition shaped by Chinese techniques and adapted local dishes such as jjajangmyeon, jjamppong and tangsuyuk.

## Taste
Common profiles include savory black-bean sauce, hot seafood broth and sweet-sour glaze, but the cuisine contains both mild and spicy dishes.

## Texture
Springy noodles, glossy sauces, crisp fried coatings and tender stir-fried ingredients appear often, without defining every dish.

## Temperature
Most signature noodle, soup and fried dishes are served hot, although delivery time can soften noodles and fried coatings.

## Satiety
Noodle and rice dishes commonly serve one person, while fried sides and combination platters are designed for sharing.

## Culture
It is strongly associated with neighborhood restaurants, delivery, family meals and Korean occasions such as moving day or graduation.

## Analogy
It is best understood as its own Korean restaurant tradition, not as a guarantee that a dish matches a recipe commonly found in China.

## Ingredients
Wheat noodles, pork, seafood, onion, fermented bean pastes and frying oils occur frequently, but no ingredient is guaranteed across the whole cuisine.

## Safety
Wheat, soy, shellfish, egg and shared-fryer exposure can occur. This cuisine node intentionally does not pass claims from Korean cuisine or assert them for every child dish.
