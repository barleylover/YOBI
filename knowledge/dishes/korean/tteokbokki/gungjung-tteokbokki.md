# Gungjung tteokbokki

## Character and experience

Gungjung tteokbokki is a non-red tteokbokki style in which rice cakes are stir-cooked with a soy-based savory seasoning and vegetables, often with beef.

It is mildly sweet and savory rather than chili-hot, with soy, aromatics and sesame often shaping the flavor.

Chewy rice cakes combine with tender vegetables and optional strips of beef.

It is served warm or hot from the pan.

It works as a substantial snack or light meal, especially when meat and vegetables are included.

## Context and comparisons

The name evokes Korean court-style cooking and distinguishes this mild soy-seasoned form from modern red tteokbokki.

It is closer to a soy-seasoned rice-cake stir-fry than to a spicy street-food stew.

## Ingredients and variations

Rice cakes and soy seasoning define the variant. Beef, mushrooms, carrot, onion and sesame oil are common but not guaranteed.

Soy is presumed present. Wheat may occur in soy sauce, and sesame is possible in finishing oil or seeds; merchant recipes must resolve a particular menu.
