# Tteokbokki

## Character and experience

Tteokbokki is a Korean dish built around chewy cylindrical rice cakes cooked in a seasoned sauce. The family includes classic red gochujang versions as well as milder and creamier variants.

The classic profile is sweet, savoury and chilli-forward. The exact sweetness, saltiness and heat vary by sauce and shop, so the family description is not a promise about one merchant's recipe.

The rice cakes should feel dense, springy and chewy. Common additions such as fish cake and cabbage add softer or lighter textures.

It is normally served hot, often directly from a simmering pan or packed warm for delivery.

A portion can work as a filling snack or a meal, depending on serving size and additions such as noodles, egg or fried sides.

## Context and comparisons

Tteokbokki is strongly associated with Korean street-food stalls and casual snack shops. It is also a familiar delivery food with many modern sauce variations.

For a first-time visitor, it can be described as very chewy rice cakes coated in a glossy sweet-savoury chilli sauce rather than as a pasta or noodle dish.

## Ingredients and variations

Rice cake is defining and gochujang is core to the classic red family. Fish cake, cabbage, scallion, boiled egg and noodles are common variations, not guaranteed contents of every menu.

Fish, soy and wheat can appear through fish cake, gochujang or other processed ingredients. This Wiki treats them as possible risks and never treats an unlisted ingredient as confirmed absent; merchant recipe and cross-contact details remain separate evidence.
