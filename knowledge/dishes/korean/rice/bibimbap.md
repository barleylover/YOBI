# Bibimbap

## Character and experience

Bibimbap is a rice bowl assembled with several seasoned vegetables and mixed at the table. Meat, egg and sauce choices depend on the menu.

It balances savory vegetables, nutty sesame notes and optional sweet-hot gochujang.

Soft rice contrasts with tender, crisp or chewy vegetables; a fried egg can add richness.

Most delivery bibimbap is warm, although some regional bowls and toppings can be cool.

A full bowl is usually a complete one-person meal, with toppings changing its richness.

## Context and comparisons

The act of mixing separate colors and textures is central to the experience, not merely presentation.

Think of a build-your-own grain bowl designed to be mixed thoroughly with sauce just before eating.

## Ingredients and variations

Rice and assorted seasoned vegetables are core. Gochujang, sesame oil, egg and meat are common rather than guaranteed.

Egg, soy, sesame and animal garnish are possible. A vegetable-forward name alone does not establish vegan status or absence of cross-contact.
