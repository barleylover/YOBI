# Korean cuisine

## Character and experience

Korean cuisine is a broad culinary tradition rather than one recipe. It includes rice meals, noodles, soups, stews, grilled dishes, fermented foods, snacks and regional specialties.

Its dishes range from mild and clean to deeply fermented, savory, sweet, smoky or spicy, so the cuisine label alone does not establish a flavor profile.

Meals often combine contrasting textures such as soft rice, crisp vegetables, chewy noodles and tender or grilled proteins, but no single texture defines the cuisine.

Hot soups and grills, room-temperature side dishes and chilled noodles all belong to Korean cuisine; the named dish determines serving temperature.

Portions range from small street snacks to complete rice-and-side-dish meals and shared feasts.

## Context and comparisons

Shared dishes, fermented condiments, seasonal ingredients and a table assembled from a staple plus accompaniments are recurring patterns, with substantial regional and household variation.

Treat Korean cuisine as a family tree of related food traditions, not as a flavor tag that can predict every dish below it.

## Ingredients and variations

Rice, grains, vegetables, soy products, seafood, meat, garlic, sesame and fermented seasonings are common across parts of the cuisine, but none is asserted for every Korean dish at this level.
