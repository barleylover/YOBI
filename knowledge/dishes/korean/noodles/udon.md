# Korean udon

## Character and experience

Korean udon is a family of thick wheat-noodle soups adapted to Korean snack-shop and casual dining menus.

The broth is usually mild, salty and savory, sometimes with pepper, chili or seafood notes.

Thick, smooth and springy noodles sit in a clear or lightly colored broth.

It is served hot.

One bowl is a moderate meal and is often paired with gimbap or fried sides.

## Context and comparisons

Korean snack shops treat udon as a familiar warming noodle category alongside ramyeon and fish-cake soup.

It is a thick-noodle broth bowl whose toppings define the named subtype.

## Ingredients and variations

Thick wheat noodles and broth define the family. Soy seasoning, fish stock, fish cake, egg and vegetables are common.

Wheat is presumed present. Soy, fish and egg are possible through broth and toppings.
